SubsurfiX

Automated workflows for subsurface geological analysis and reservoir modeling, from well-log QC to flow and heat simulation, with a Rust engine and a Python interface.

  • GitHub
  • Zenodo
  • PyPI
  • conda-forge

Overview

SubsurfiX is developed to automate workflows for subsurface geological analysis and reservoir modeling, including (1) machine-learning (ML) or statistically assisted well-log quality control (QC) and data-gap prediction, (2) core-to-log calibration and 3D property modeling, (3) stratigraphic correlation and top picking, and (4) flow and heat simulations for injection–production purposes. The engine is written in Rust and wrapped in a Python skin.

Examples

Each step has an executed notebook on open data from the Kansas Geological Survey and FORCE 2020. The flow examples (09, 10 and 14) run on the whole 11 × 11 km Kansas model.

Key features

01

Log clean-up and gap filling

Read LAS files and well surveys, mark bad sections, and fill gaps with rules and LightGBM. Every value carries a code that says whether it was measured or filled.

02

Well correlation

Match the same beds from well to well automatically, solving all wells together.

03

Grids and properties

Regular grids, or corner-point grids with faults read from GRDECL files. Fill them with porosity and rock type by kriging, simulation or machine learning, with P10/P50/P90 cases.

04

Flow and heat (FIM-OBL)

A fully implicit finite-volume solver for water, water–oil, oil–gas and CO₂–brine, plus heat, on regular and faulted grids. Flow diagnostics show where the fluid goes and how long it takes.

05

CO₂ storage screening

A vertical-equilibrium model, after the MRST co2lab papers, that runs in seconds and reports structural and residual trapping. Example 13 compares it with FIM-OBL on the same case.

06

MODFLOW 6 and data exchange

Groundwater flow and heat through MODFLOW 6 and FloPy, Leapfrog through OMF files, and OpenGeoSys mesh files (.vtu).

Installation

pip install subsurfix

Needs Python 3.12 or newer. Ready-built wheels are for Apple Silicon Macs. On other systems pip builds from source, which needs Rust, a C/C++ compiler and libclang. Examples 09 and 10 also need the MODFLOW 6 program.
