SubsurfiX

Automated workflows for subsurface geological analysis and reservoir modeling, from well-log QC to flow and heat simulation, with a Rust core and a Python interface.

  • GitHub
  • Zenodo

Overview

SubsurfiX is developed to automate workflows for subsurface geological analysis and reservoir modeling, including (1) machine-learning (ML) or statistically assisted well-log quality control (QC) and data-gap prediction, (2) core-to-log calibration and 3D property modeling, (3) Dynamic Time Warping (DTW) assisted stratigraphic correlation between wells, and (4) flow and heat simulations for injection–production purposes. The core is written in Rust and wrapped in a Python interface.

Version 0.2.0 adds an experimental finite-element (FEM) solver for coupled water flow and heat beside the finite-volume (FVM) solvers, an FVM-versus-FEM comparison on the Kansas model, and a Kansas CO₂-into-brine comparison with and without two branching faults. The package is under active development; many functions are still experimental and not fully validated.

Examples

Each step has an executed notebook on open data from the Kansas Geological Survey and FORCE 2020. The flow examples run on the whole 11 × 11 km Kansas model.

Flow and heat methods

The finite-volume method (FVM) and the finite-element method (FEM) are the two spatial methods. SubsurfiX provides two FVM solvers: an open-DARTS-inspired fully implicit solver with operator-based linearization (FIM-OBL) for water, water–oil, oil–gas and CO₂–brine with heat, and a MODFLOW-inspired control-volume finite-difference solver, translated into Rust, for single-fluid pressure and heat. The official USGS MODFLOW 6 program is a separate, optional external check. The FEM solver is an experimental OpenGeoSys-style continuous Galerkin solver for coupled water flow and heat on unfaulted grids. Multiphase flow and faulted models remain FVM-only.

Meshes are corner-point grids, including faulted grids read from GRDECL files, and conforming hexahedral bulk meshes shared by FVM and FEM.

Key features

01

Log clean-up and gap filling

Read LAS files and well surveys, mark bad sections, and fill gaps with rules and LightGBM. Every value carries a code that says whether it was measured or filled.

02

Well correlation

Match the same beds from well to well automatically, solving all wells together.

03

Grids and properties

Regular grids, corner-point grids with faults read from GRDECL files, and hexahedral bulk meshes. Fill them with porosity and rock type by kriging, simulation or machine learning, with P10/P50/P90 cases.

04

Flow and heat (FVM)

Two finite-volume solvers: fully implicit FIM-OBL for water, water–oil, oil–gas and CO₂–brine, plus heat, on regular and faulted grids, and a MODFLOW-inspired solver for single-fluid pressure and heat. Flow diagnostics show where the fluid goes and how long it takes.

05

Water and heat (FEM, experimental)

An OpenGeoSys-style continuous Galerkin finite-element solver for coupled water flow and heat on unfaulted grids, using the same water, rock and wells as FVM so the two compare directly.

06

CO₂ screening and data exchange

A vertical-equilibrium CO₂ model after MRST that runs in seconds; optional MODFLOW 6 checks through FloPy; Leapfrog through OMF files and OpenGeoSys mesh files (.vtu).

Installation

pip install subsurfix

Needs Python 3.12 or newer. Ready-built wheels are for Apple Silicon Macs. On other systems pip builds from source, which needs Rust, a C/C++ compiler and libclang. Examples 09 and 10 also need the MODFLOW 6 program.
